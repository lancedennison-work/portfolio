Grouping TL;DR

Purpose: when FindPath hits an obstacle, find all the touching or nearby obstacles that form one connected mass, so the agent avoids the whole cluster instead of just one piece.

Trigger: runs once per obstacle, only when tangents resolved and the obstacle isn't already in a cached group.

How it works:

  1. Start from the primary obstacle's hull and its two pivots. The FindPath hit point is the loop-closure marker.
  2. Walk the cluster's outer edge twice, once CCW from the Right pivot and once CW from the Left pivot. Both walks share one cluster.
  3. Each step sweeps along the edge (and a buffer past) looking for the next obstacle touching it.
  4. Nothing found: move to the next vertex. A full lap ends the walk.

Result:

  • More than one member: hull the union, find tangents, and replace the footprint and pivots.

Grouping; long version

  1. Trigger — runs inside FindPath when tangents are resolved and the primary object isn't already in a cached group.
    1. It captures the primary's hull and pivots, and sets InitialCollisionPoint to the FindPath hit point.
    2. It walks twice: Right pivot with bForward=true (CCW) and Left pivot with bForward=false (CW). Both walks share one Cluster and one GroupPoints.
      1. This avoids some orphan vertices that can be skipped from a single chain, and smooths out some of the lingering kinks in the system.
  2. Outcome
    1. More than one member: the union is hulled and tangents are found on the merged shape. If both work, the footprint and pivots are replaced and the group is registered. A merge failure keeps the primary-only result.
    2. One member: cached as isolated.
  3. ChainDiscoverObstacles (per-walk)
    1. It tracks the current hull, pivot, owner and starting pivot, with RemainingHops as a runaway backstop. A degenerate corner ends the walk.
    2. Hits are ignored for self, the player and the current object.
    3. Step 1 is a box sweep down the outside of the edge, starting TraceStartHysteresisUU along it.
    4. Step 2 runs only if step 1 missed. It sweeps one buffer length past the edge endpoint, outside then inside (buffer length capped to the object's inward depth), and outside wins if both are hits.
    5. Nothing found means advance to the next neighbor vertex in the chain order. A full lap ends the walk.
  4. Hit handling
    1. Within LoopClosureTolerance of InitialCollisionPoint, the loop is closed and the walk stops. A hit on a character also stops it.
    2. To avoid cases of nested objects within the previous, PrevObject uses an exit flag: if set, resume tracing PrevObject and swap current/previous. Otherwise update the flag from whether Neighbour left PrevObject's hull, and treat the hit as a miss.
    3. Another cluster member gets a turn test: a wrong turn is a miss, and a valid turn is a wraparound that stops.
    4. An actual new object is first footprinted at the touch point and added to the cluster. A round one hands off to RouteAroundCircle and waits for its return.
    5. With fewer than 3 hull points, the walk stops.
    6. Otherwise the continuation vertex is the nearest vertex, or the stopping point spliced in if it lies on one of that vertex's edges and isn't within tolerance of either endpoint.
  5. RouteAroundCircle
    1. It caches the circle's neighbours using an overlap of its radius plus half the buffer, excluding characters.
    2. It picks the nearest unclaimed neighbour object to the entry point, skipping anything at InitialCollisionPoint.
    3. An older-group neighbour is absorbed and the walk stops. A round neighbour recurses.
    4. A cornered neighbour gets its support vertex picked along the circle's tangent direction, then the walk hands back to the edge walk.
