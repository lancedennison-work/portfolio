Class Split

Player

Functional Crosshair

The key (new) feature that the PlayerAgent needs—that isn't cut out from the pre-split Agent class—is a crosshair/reticle. Currently, how the AAgent shoots is: it draws a trace hitscan out using the muzzle as the start of the vector, then it travels (firing range) down the camera forward vector to get the trace end, and hits anything along that new path. In third person games, there is always the issue of the camera and the gun being in very different places, and the closer an object is to the gun, the more extreme that difference becomes. So, you have to find how to balance between the bullets—at least feeling like they are—coming out of the gun, and going to where you are aiming.

Sometimes this means they show you where you are aiming, and where you are going to hit, like in Helldivers 2:

To test my options and find how I want the hitscan-behavior/reticle-placement to function, I needed a way to probe in the same direction relative to the player's weapon every tick.

Manually updating the direction based on the player's movement and rotation every tick, to test for collisions, seemed excessive. Especially, when the camera boom does exactly what I am looking for.

The camera boom uses a Spring Arm Component: it has a fixed path, with a maximum range, and changes its length depending on physical collision; it's just normally supposed to be behind the player. So, I socketed one to the weapon muzzle, and one to the follow camera and flipped them around.

Voilà.

This perfectly creates a no-fuss, consistent angle, with linear probing at fixed maximum distance, every tick. Now I can see the path of the gun, where it is hitting and where the camera is hitting (it's always pointing at the center of the screen). I then added a sweeping line between the end probe sockets of the arms (where they are colliding with something), and if an Agent collides with that path, I get a blended target vector for the pseudo-midpoint.

To test which one I liked, I chose one as the winner, each tick: whichever collision was hitting the closest agent, is the one that the gun used for Fire(). That way, I could test all three options and together as a fourth, secret, option.

The 3 options.

  • Red: Trace from muzzle to Camera
    • Pros: Highest accuracy; its target location doesn't bob around from the idle animation, and is always at the center of the screen so you don't have to coax the camera into lining up the hitscan
    • Cons: Looks weird if you can see the line the closer you are to the target as it skews further away from the barrel direction, and when you get the barrel close enough to something it kind of breaks.
    • Accurate past close range for both hipfire and aiming
  • Blue: Trace from muzzle straight out
    • Pros: Works well up close, and is animation-accurate.
    • Cons: Moves around a lot the farther away you are aiming due to aforementioned accuracy with the idle animation. Primitive rotational movement and no IK on the arms means the trace end is almost never at the center of the screen.
    • Accurate at very close range, more accurate during aiming due to camera offset.
  • Purple: Trace sweep between arms
    • Pros: Makes up for the inconsistent distance weaknesses of the bounding arms.
    • Cons: Though it works well to balance at long range since the angle gets very close to the red, it does not equally balance the middle distances
    • Maybe the least accurate at this stage, very hard to balance just right.

note

The real solution is animation / moving the gun to point in the direction of the "Red" option. But this was a fun test to set up.

Current Winner: Muzzle to Camera Arm Probe; the other two have been banished to the future iteration shadow realm.

Since I already have an object always at the center of the screen, the debug sphere for the camera-anchored arm has been promoted to pseudo reticle/crosshair! Congrats!

Enemy

Behaviors

Currently, enemies have four behavior tree states: Patrol, Investigate, Attack, and Die. While patrolling, they move towards their next patrol point, then to the next once they reach it. When investigating, they go to the last place they saw the player, if they can no longer see them. When attacking, they face the player and shoot. When they die, well… they're dead.

Behavior Modifiers

Alarmed is a behavior condition; the reason it is not a primary state is because it alters their global behavior, an enemy can be alarmed in any of previously mentioned states.

What it does

When an enemy is Alarmed, they are actively looking, instead of passively guarding. Instead of just keeping an eye out on their "post" (close surroundings), they are searching for an enemy that isn't close to them since someone else spotted them.

Additionally, when on alarm, everyone knows that something is going on, so the enemies will have greater tolerances for searching when alarmed.

Why?

This provides a more realistic sentry behavior. If you are guarding the back of a building, but someone near the front raises the alarm, you are going to search a much more distant area, requiring you to focus, tightening your (active) field of vision as you strain to look farther away. Of course, you aren't a camera, you aren't zooming in; but you are switching from a passive scan to a focused search, so your brain will shift priority from your peripheral vision. Also, if someone had raised the alarm, you are going to look around for a lot longer than a "hey what was that" search.
