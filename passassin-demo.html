step 1

Intro

Project Goal

The aim of this project is to make an ambitious Unreal Third-Person Stealth-Shooter game from the ground up, only using blueprint nodes where C++ fails to be useful (like ABP graphs). I am going to go off my first guess to create novel (to me) solutions for the first iterations of the classes, and refine the technical elements towards my goal designs or game-design-standard solutions I remember.

Pitch

You play as an assassin that uses the ability to phase through solid objects. The tradeoff; you can't see through those walls, so you have to use information from other senses to know exactly where to pop out behind your target, instead of out in the open with a bunch of bad guys looking right at you.

Inspirations

I have a few different games that will guide the direction of my design goals.

Stealth: Dishonored

I want a complex stealth system: Enemies that--

  • Can patrol
  • Have a vision cone
  • Vision state that builds a tolerance value up to spotting the character
    • And back down if not triggered
  • Attack when able
  • Investigate when vision fully gained, then lost
  • Goes back to patrol when found nothing
  • Alerted mode when someone raises the alarm

Enemy Behavior: Halo

I want flavorful enemy behaviors; which the Halo franchise famously has. Each enemy type has so much personality baked into them through their behavior tree. This is well supported by the amazing animation of each "class" that sells the experiential narrative that--the Covenant as a prime example--are distinct races in a strict caste system socially and militarily.

Group Behavior: The Last of Us

I want a system to allow for cooperative enemy behavior--like how enemies can "bark" to others, and work together to locate you, pincer you, and otherwise act like people who use the numbers they have as an advantage. This differs from many stealth games which, a lot of the time, seem to have you almost magically globally visible. WIth a more nuanced system, like TLoU, actors have to share information for them to accurately pinpoint your location.

step 4

Player

Functional Crosshair

The--key, new--feature that the PlayerAgent needs---that isn't cut out from the pre-split Agent class, is a UI crosshair. Currently the way the AAgent shoots: it draws a trace hitscan out using the muzzle as the start of the vector, then it uses the firing range down the camera forward as the trace end. In third person games, the camera... [you can see past where gun can shoot].

[solution]

I set up some tools for testing and drew debug lines based on them to use for iterating. To test my options and find how I want the hitscan-behavior/reticle-placement, I needed a way to probe in the same direction relative to the player's weapon every tick.

Manually updating on each tick the direction each based on the player's movement and rotation, to test seemed excessive. Especially when the camera boom does exactly what I am looking for. The spring arm component has a fixed path, it's just normally supposed to be behind the player. So, I socketed one to the weapon muzzle, and one to the follow camera and flipped them around.

This perfectly creates a no-fuss, consistent angle, every-tick, with linear probing at fixed maximum distance. Now I can see where the gun is pointing towards and hitting and where the camera--always pointing at the center of the screen--is hitting. I then added a sweeping line between the end probe sockets of the arms (where they are colliding with something), and if an Agent collides with that path, I get a blended target vector for the pseudo-midpoint.

all three at once test description picks whichever is hitting the closest agent

all 3 hitcan debugs

This gives 3 options.

  • Red: Trace from muzzle to Camera
    • Pros: Highest accuracy; its target location doesn't bob around from the idle animation, and is always at the center of the screen so you don't have to coax the camera into lining up the hitscan
    • Cons: Looks weird if you can see the line the closer you are to the target as it skews further away from the barrel direction, and when you get the barrel close enough to something it kind of breaks.
    • Accurate past close range for both hipfire and aiming
  • Blue: Trace from muzzle straight out
    • Pros: Works well up close, and is animation-accurate.
    • Cons: Moves around a lot the farther away you are aiming due to aforementioned accuracy with the idle animation. Primitive rotational movement and no IK on the arms means the trace end is almost never at the center of the screen.
    • Accurate at very close range, more accurate during aiming due to camera offset
  • Purple: Trace sweep between arms
    • Pros: Makes up for the inconsistent distance weaknesses of the bounding arms.
    • Cons: Though it works well to balance at long range since the angle gets very close to the red, it does not equally balance the middle distances
    • Maybe the least accurate at this stage, very hard to balance just right
